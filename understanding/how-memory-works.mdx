---
title: "How memory works"
description: "What MindMap remembers across conversations, how to teach it, and how to correct it."
---

MindMap keeps a memory that carries across every conversation. Tell it something once and it uses that context the next time you talk to it.

## What it remembers

MindMap saves things you tell it on purpose, and it picks up patterns from your conversations over time. For example:

- Your name, what you do, where you are based
- Your preferences ("I like bullet points, not walls of text")
- Your ongoing projects and goals
- People, places, and things you mention often
- How you communicate and the tone you prefer
- Deadlines and plans you have talked about

## How to teach it things

Tell it something directly and it saves it:

- "Remember that my business partner is named Kim."
- "I prefer short, direct answers."
- "My biggest goal this year is to get 20 new clients by December."

You can also talk to it the way you would talk to a person. Mention your schedule, your clients, or your week, and it picks up on those things over time.

## What it does not do on its own

MindMap will not know something unless you told it or it came up in a conversation.

- **It does not read your email or calendar unless a connector is on.** See [Connect an app](/guides/5-connect-an-app).
- **It does not know what is on your computer.** A file on your desktop does not exist for MindMap until you upload it. See [Cloud, not local](/understanding/cloud-not-local).

## Fixing something it remembered wrong

Correct it the way you would correct a colleague:

"Actually, my shop moved to the new address. Update that."

"I changed my mind on that. I would rather get longer, detailed answers now."

MindMap updates what it knows. You can review and search what has been saved on the **Memory** page.

## Memory across different chats

Memory is shared across your whole workspace and is not tied to one chat thread. Start a new conversation, or switch between Chat, the Canvas, and a Space, and it is the same assistant with the same memory. Each of those surfaces keeps its own conversation history, so an empty-looking chat means that thread has no history yet, not that memory is gone. If you also reach MindMap through a connected messaging channel, memory still flows to the same place.

If it seems to have forgotten you, see [When something breaks](/guides/10-when-something-breaks).
