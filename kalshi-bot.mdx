An event-market trading bot. Research and forecast agents decide direction, a risk engine decides the money with hard gates and position sizing, and it pings you when a bet stages. Paper mode first, your money and your rules when you go live.

## The loop

Every day the bot works event markets on [Kalshi](https://kalshi.com), the regulated US prediction exchange, through a pipeline where each agent has one job:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Scan">
    A scheduled scan pulls the day's markets and filters down to candidates worth a real look: enough volume, enough time, a question that research can actually move.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Research">
    A research agent digs into each candidate: news, primary sources, base rates. It comes back with evidence, not a hunch.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Forecast">
    A forecast agent turns the evidence into a probability and compares it against the market's price. No edge, no trade.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Critique">
    A critique agent attacks the thesis before money does. If the case survives its own devil's advocate, it moves on.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Risk engine">
    Code, not a model, decides the money: position sizing, exposure caps, daily limits. An LLM never touches the wallet directly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Alert">
    A staged bet lands in Discord with the thesis, the probability, the price, and the size. You see exactly why before anything happens.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The risk engine is the boss

The design rule that matters most: agents propose, the risk engine disposes. Hard position-size caps, exposure limits, and balance checks live in plain code with no way for a persuasive forecast to talk past them. If a gate fails, the trade dies, no matter how good the thesis looked.

## Human in the loop

The bot runs its research and staging on schedule, but real money moves on approval. Paper mode proved the pipeline first, and live trading keeps a person on the trigger. It's a research department that never sleeps, not a slot machine.

## The stack

Claude agents for research, forecasting, and critique. A scheduler for the daily scan. The Kalshi API for markets and orders. Discord for alerts. A risk module in plain code holding the keys.

## Status

Running daily scans with staged alerts. Live-money automation stays deliberately conservative: the gates exist because event markets punish overconfidence, and the bot is built to know that.
