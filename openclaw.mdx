A private AI assistant that actually does things: job agents, email, research, and messengers, all running in your own workspace. I set it up and show you how to drive it. Talk to it from WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord, or email, connect it to your tools, and let it run tasks on a schedule.

## What you get

Your own instance. Not a shared bot, not a seat in someone else's tenant: a workspace that runs for you, holds your keys, and answers to nobody else's prompts.

- **Your channels**: WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord, or plain email. You message it like a person and it answers like one that can actually do the work.
- **Your tools**: calendars, inboxes, files, web search, and whatever else your day runs on, connected with your own credentials.
- **Schedules**: standing jobs that fire while you sleep. A morning digest, a nightly check, a weekly report. You describe it once and it keeps happening.
- **Memory**: it remembers your context between conversations, so Tuesday's assistant knows what you told it Monday.

## What people actually use it for

The honest list, from real setups: a job-hunt agent that watches postings and drafts applications, email triage that surfaces what matters and drafts replies, research runs that come back with sources instead of vibes, and reminder-and-schedule glue for a life that has too many moving parts.

## How the setup goes

<Steps>
  <Step title="A short call">
    You tell me what you want it doing. Twenty minutes, plain language, no spec document.
  </Step>
  <Step title="I stand it up">
    Your instance, your channels wired, your first tools connected. You get login details and a working assistant, not a to-do list.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The handoff">
    A walkthrough of how to drive it: what to ask, how to add tools, how to set schedules. Recorded so you can rewatch.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You drive">
    It's yours. I stay reachable for questions and can add capabilities as you grow into it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Straight answers

It runs isolated, so your data and keys stay in your workspace. You can see everything it does and everything it remembers. And if you grow past a personal assistant into wanting dashboards, CRM, and a full approval-gated workspace, [MindMap](/mindmap) is the bigger sibling and the migration is a conversation, not a rebuild.
