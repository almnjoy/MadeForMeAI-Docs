The whole point of Market Forge is that the discipline lives in code. Here is exactly
what that means.

## The pipeline a trade must survive

<Steps>
  <Step title="Screener">
    The day's biggest movers come from Alpaca. Nothing is trusted yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verification">
    Every percentage is recomputed: current price from the **live tape**, prior close
    from the last completed session's real bar. Stale prints, par-priced warrants, and
    unadjusted splits get corrected - and if the real move no longer clears the
    threshold, the name is dropped entirely rather than shown as a fake mover.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scoring">
    An LLM reads the headlines plus any Reddit chatter and returns a 0-100 conviction
    score, a signal-or-noise verdict, and a one-line reason.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Gates">
    Every gate below must pass. Any failure, or any missing data, blocks the trade.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Entry with an exit">
    A whole-share market buy, then the fill is confirmed, then a GTC trailing stop is
    armed. The exit exists before you look away.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The gates

| Gate | Default | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Master switch | off | Live auto-entries need **two** flags on, on purpose |
| Conviction | score >= 85 and verdict "signal" | only high-conviction catalysts |
| Data trust | verified move, not an IPO first-session | never trade a number we could not check |
| Price floor | >= $3.00 | keeps the bot out of low-float spiker junk |
| Size | $50 per trade | small while learning |
| Frequency | 2 entries per day | no machine-gunning a bad day |
| Total exposure | $150 of auto capital | a hard ceiling regardless of ideas |
| Concurrency | never adds to a held symbol | no accidental averaging down |
| Kill switch | drawdown / daily-loss breaker | trips and nothing enters |

All of them **fail closed**: if a value cannot be read, the answer is no.

## Exits, and why trailing

Default exit is a **GTC trailing stop** at 10%. The stop follows the high-water mark
and never moves down, so a runner keeps running and a fader gets cut.

That directly fixes the most common beginner mistake: watching a winner round-trip.

<Warning>
Two honest caveats. A tight trail on a volatile name will shake you out mid-run - the
width is a real decision, not a default to ignore. And a stop trigger fires a market
order, so on a thin name in a fast drop the fill can be worse than the stop level.
</Warning>

The alternative mode is a fixed bracket: take-profit and stop-loss attached at entry.
Set `RADAR_AUTO_EXIT=bracket` if you prefer defined targets to a trailing exit.

## Going live

Live trading is off by default and takes deliberate steps.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Trade paper until the journal says you are ready">
    Not until it feels good - until the replay shows a process you would repeat.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add live keys">
    Put your Alpaca **live** key pair in `ALPACA_LIVE_KEY_ID` and
    `ALPACA_LIVE_SECRET_KEY`, then set `STOCK_ENV=live`. Paper and live keys are
    separate settings so one can never be used as the other.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Arm the autos, if you want them">
    Live auto-entries require **both** `RADAR_AUTO_EXECUTE=true` and
    `LIVE_AUTO_ENABLED=true`. Manual trading through the ticket works either way.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Restart the app">
    Settings are read at startup. Restart so the engine picks them up, then confirm
    the header shows **LIVE** in red and the auto chip reads what you expect.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Worst realistic day at the defaults: two $50 entries both stopping out is roughly a
$10 loss. That is the point of starting there.

## Tuning with your copilot

On the RULES tab, click any setting. It drafts a question that gets you an explanation
of the trade-off, a suggestion checked against your `memory.md`, and the exact steps to
apply it. Change one thing at a time and let the journal tell you whether it helped.

<Note>
Ideas worth revisiting once you have data: trail width by volatility instead of a flat
10%, a time-of-day filter, scaling out half a position, and feeding settled trades back
into the scorer as lessons.
</Note>

## What Market Forge will never do

- Enable live trading on its own
- Place a trade the copilot was not explicitly told to place
- Send your keys anywhere - they live in `bot/.env` on your disk
- Hide what it did - every action lands in the journal
